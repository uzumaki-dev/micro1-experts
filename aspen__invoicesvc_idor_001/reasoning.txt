Threat model: aspen__invoicesvc_idor_001

The invoicesvc service represents a SaaS billing platform. Invoices contain
commercially sensitive data: internal cost codes, discount structures, contract
attachment bytes, and private memo fields. Each invoice is associated with a
specific owner (the user who created it).

The IDOR vulnerability: none of the read-path endpoints check the caller's
identity against the invoice's owner_id. Any user with a valid X-User-Id header
can enumerate invoice IDs and extract full invoice payloads, line-item cost codes,
and attachment bytes. The duplicate endpoint compounds this by copying private
fields (internal_notes, attachments) into a new invoice owned by the attacker,
enabling a laundering chain: duplicate → PATCH/share/export.

Why test-authoring is the right evaluation:
- The scenario involves multiple non-obvious leak surfaces (list, search, export,
  line-items, attachments, path traversal, three laundering chains) that only a
  careful threat-model-aware agent would discover.
- Weaker models tend to write status-code-only assertions (403 checks) that miss
  the actual data leak. Sentinel content inspection is the correct approach.
- The dual-contract requirement (catch the leak + preserve legitimate access)
  requires the agent to reason about both attack and regression simultaneously.

Discrimination design:
- Saturated items (rub_001, rub_002, rub_012, rub_013, rub_016): basic IDOR
  awareness + anti-overblock for owner/admin — any capable model catches these.
- Mid-tier items (rub_003, rub_004, rub_005, rub_014, rub_015): secondary
  surfaces (search, line-items, json export) and collaborator/public-visibility
  guards — require broader threat-model induction.
- Hard items (rub_006, rub_007, rub_008, rub_009, rub_017): CSV inspection,
  attachment download, path traversal, duplicate+PATCH laundering, and the
  comprehensive sentinel sweep — require format-aware and chained-flow reasoning.
- Top-of-frontier items (rub_010, rub_011): duplicate+share and duplicate+export
  laundering chains — only frontier models reason about multi-step laundering.
